Production Cost

Fixed and variable costs, average and marginal cost, and how costs behave in the short run and the long run.

01 · What counts as cost

Explicit and implicit costs

Explicit costsMoney actually paid: wages, rent, materials, electricity.
Implicit costsOpportunity cost of the owner's own time, money and property.

Accounting profit = Revenue − Explicit costs

Economic profit = Revenue − Explicit − Implicit costs

02 · Time horizon

03 · Short-run costs

Fixed and variable costs

Fixed cost · FC

Do not change with output

Rent, insurance, equipment loan. Paid even when Q = 0.
Variable cost · VC

Change with output

Flour, electricity, wages for extra workers.

TC = FC + VC

05 · Cost per unit

Average costs

AFC

FC ÷ Q

Always falls as output grows.
AVC

VC ÷ Q

Falls first, then rises.
ATC

TC ÷ Q

Equals AFC + AVC. U-shaped.

Example: at Q = 5, ATC = 320 ÷ 5 = 64 thousand sum per batch.

06 · The extra unit

Marginal cost

Marginal cost is the additional cost of producing one more unit of output.

MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ

Why MC falls firstWorkers specialize and the fixed oven is used more fully.
Why MC rises laterDiminishing marginal returns: too many workers share one oven.

08 · Long run

Economies of scale

LRAC falls

Economies of scale

Bulk buying, specialization, better machines.
LRAC flat

Constant returns

Doubling inputs exactly doubles output.
LRAC rises

Diseconomies of scale

The firm is too big to manage and coordinate well.
09 · Conclusion

Key takeaways

1

Economic cost = explicit + implicit costs.

2

In the short run, TC = FC + VC.

3

MC rises due to diminishing returns and crosses ATC at its minimum.

4

In the long run, size matters: economies and diseconomies of scale.
